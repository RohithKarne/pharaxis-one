## 1. Sowmya — the question wording

**Locked principle: event first, causality never.** The reporter is not qualified to attribute cause, and asking them to invites both under-reporting ("I don't know if it was the drug, so I'll say no") and false attribution.

### The question

> **Did anyone become unwell, or have an unexpected medical problem, after using the product?**
>
> ○ Yes ○ No
>
> *Helper text: This includes anything you did not expect — however minor, and whether or not you think the product caused it.*

### Why this wording and not the obvious alternatives

| Rejected | Reason |
|---|---|
| "Did you experience a **side effect**?" | "Side effect" presumes attribution. A reporter who is unsure answers No |
| "Did you have a **health problem**?" | Too broad — catches every pre-existing condition, floods the queue |
| "Did you experience an **adverse event**?" | Regulatory jargon. Most reporters do not know the term |
| "…**caused by** the product?" | Asks for a causality judgement. Clinically wrong to delegate to the reporter |

**"anyone"** rather than "you" — the reporter is frequently not the patient (carer, clinician, relative). Three of the four portal form types are routinely filed on behalf of someone else.

**"after using"** — temporal, not causal. This is the standard pharmacovigilance framing.

**The helper text does the real work.** "However minor, and whether or not you think the product caused it" is the sentence that converts a hesitant No into a Yes. Without it the question under-performs.

### Clinical constraint on the follow-up box

If Yes opens a detail box, it must be **optional**. A mandatory narrative is a barrier — a reporter who cannot articulate it will change their answer to No to get past the form. **A Yes with no detail is still a valuable flag.**

## 2. Saad — user stories and acceptance criteria

### Story 1 — Portal visitor is asked about harm

> As a portal visitor filing a non-AE submission, I am asked whether anyone became unwell, so that a possible adverse event is not lost because I filed under the wrong form type.

**Acceptance criteria**

| # | Criterion |
|---|---|
| 1.1 | The question appears on `medical_inquiry`, `product_complaint`, `other_inquiry`. **Not** on `adverse_event` |
| 1.2 | The question is **mandatory**. Submitting without answering returns 400 with a field-level message |
| 1.3 | The question appears **regardless of client form configuration**. A client cannot remove, hide, or reorder it out of view |
| 1.4 | Answering **Yes** reveals an **optional** free-text box. Submission succeeds with the box empty |
| 1.5 | The question renders in the client's configured language alongside existing fields |
| 1.6 | Answer and any detail text are stored with the submission and are visible to admin |
| 1.7 | The visitor sees **no indication** the answer raised a flag — no warning, no different confirmation. The reference number and confirmation are unchanged |

### Story 2 — A Yes raises a safety review task

> As a client safety reviewer, I get a task for every submission where harm was reported, so I can assess it before it is treated as an ordinary enquiry.

**Acceptance criteria**

| # | Criterion |
|---|---|
| 2.1 | `Yes` creates **exactly one** review task per submission |
| 2.2 | `No` creates **no** task |
| 2.3 | The task **cannot be closed without an outcome** being selected |
| 2.4 | Two outcomes exist and are **stored distinctly**: `reviewed_not_ae` (clinical, named safety role only) and `cleared_administrative` (any admin, **reason mandatory**, minimum 10 characters) |
| 2.5 | Closing records who, when, outcome and reason — retrievable for audit |
| 2.6 | The task appears on **both** the submissions list (badge) and a dedicated safety queue |
| 2.7 | Task state never blocks or delays the submission's sync |

### Story 3 — Sync is unaffected by the flag *(phase 1 scope)*

> As a client, my submissions continue to reach MIMS exactly as before, so adding a safety control does not disturb an integration that already works.

**Acceptance criteria**

| # | Criterion |
|---|---|
| 3.1 | The MIMS payload is **byte-identical** with and without a flag. Phase 1 does not transmit it |
| 3.2 | **`other_inquiry` never syncs** — flag or no flag. CP-only, unchanged |
| 3.3 | **The submission type is unchanged by the flag.** An `MI` stays `MI` |
| 3.4 | A client with no integration configured keeps the flag locally, no error, no lost task |
| 3.5 | Task state never blocks, delays, or fails the sync |

> **Phase 2 — [MIMS-64](https://rohithkarne.atlassian.net/browse/MIMS-64).** Transmitting the flag needs a MIMS-side schema and API change; `POST /api/v1/cases` has nowhere to put it today (§5.1). Item 2's locked "flag travels to MIMS" **stands as a decision** and is deferred, not withdrawn.

### Explicit non-goals

- No classification, inference, or text analysis of any kind. **Option D replaced the classifier — see PD-2 comment 3.**
- No reclassification. Ever.
- Chatbox is **out of scope** (Rohith, 4th Aug).
- No alerting to the portal visitor.

## 5. Anirudh — mandatory-field injection mechanism

Server-injecting a required field into a client-configured form is a **new pattern in CP Portal** (Varun). Writing it as a mechanism, not a special case.

**Rule:** a system-managed field is one the platform injects and the client cannot remove, hide, rename to nothing, or reorder out of view. It carries `system_managed: 1`.

**Where injection happens:** exactly one place — the forms endpoint (`content.js`). **Not** in the frontend, which would let a modified client bypass it, and **not** scattered per form type.

**The invariant to test, not just document:** a client config that contains a colliding `field_key` must not suppress or duplicate the system field. The system field always wins. That is test T1.4 and it is the one that matters — everything else is convenience.

**Cross-app note:** MIMS has its own field-configuration layer (`field_setup`, 792 rows) with a comparable tenant-isolation rule. **Same problem, two solutions.** Not worth unifying today; worth recording so it is a known divergence rather than a discovered one.

### 5.1 Check RESOLVED — **no, MIMS has nowhere to put it**

`POST /api/v1/cases` (`apps/mims/backend/routes/apiPlatform.js:138-170`) accepts `case_type`, `intake_channel`, `reference`, `description`, `priority` and the three intake sub-objects. The INSERT at `:168` writes ten columns. **None carries a review flag.**

MIMS's possible-AE handling lives entirely on the **`inquiries`** table — the email inbox — via `triage_state` / `routing_reason` / admin notifications (`emailCaseImportService.js:326-349`). It was built for inbound email and does not generalise to cases.

**My assumption was wrong**, and it was an assumption, not a finding — I read "MIMS handles possible-AE" and took it to generalise. Raised as [MIMS-64](https://rohithkarne.atlassian.net/browse/MIMS-64).

`priority` (`:149`) is the closest available field and **must not be used** — it means urgency, any user can change it, and it does not answer *"show me which cases arrived flagged."*
